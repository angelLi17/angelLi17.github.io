Being so close to home for college is hard for my parents and myself because they know they can find me or I can go back so easily. While I appreciate the safety of having home nearby, it definitely propagates the stage in-between childhood and adulthood. But I know I'll never get so much love and babying from anyone else and that I will not have my parents forever. It did make me reflect on the other possibilities open to me at the time, like Johns Hopkins or Georgia Tech or UCLA.

**On Re-learning**

I think a lot of it is because I'm worried about maximizing my freshmen year, and I regret that I couldn't help but start neglecting things like physical health or mental health again. I thought I learned this lesson in junior year of high school, I mean it was in my college essays, but I guess lessons can be re-learned. Coincidentally, all three techs I'm taking this year are kind of revisiting and relearning topics I've taken in high school: physics e&m, linear algebra, dsa. I'm picking up stuff I missed the first time round, like the line density theory which is the origin of Gauss' law. I hate rewatching and rereading shows and books because I already know what happens, but in practice learning things a second is almost always valuable.

**On social life**

I get energy from being alone. I don't find myself seeking new social circles, and I think that's totally okay. The groups I'm a part of, my friend group from the summer, my church community, Cal Hacks, course staff, are sufficient for me. I've noticed it's much easier with a group of people instead of many single connections I have that require so much effort to reach out to every individual — high school friends, summer camp friends, etc. College and school is really the thing that binds me to a lot of people I know, and without the structure of having classes together or studying or eating together I'm not sure if we'd stay together, but I definitely will put in the effort to. But really, having people drift in and out is normal! 

A lot of the groups that I have joined have been saying the same thing to me. "We want to get to know you better!" But what does that even mean? Is it just spending more time together? I don't really know what it means to be "known" to others, since I guess that requires knowing myself first.
